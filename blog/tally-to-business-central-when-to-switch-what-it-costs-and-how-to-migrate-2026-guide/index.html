Tally to Business Central: When to Switch, What It Costs and How to Migrate (2026 Guide)

Tally is where most Indian businesses start, and for good reason. It is affordable, fast for accounting and GST returns, and every accountant knows it. But as a company adds plants, warehouses, branches and people, the same Tally setup starts to slow growth down.

This guide explains when moving from Tally to Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central makes sense, what it costs in India, and how to migrate without disrupting your books.

Quick answer: Move from Tally to Business Central when you need more than accounting: real-time inventory across locations, manufacturing and BOMs, approvals, multi-company consolidation, or reports that do not depend on Excel. A typical migration for a small or mid-sized Indian company takes 8 to 16 weeks and moves your masters, open balances and open documents, while full Tally history stays available for reference.

8 Signs Your Business Has Outgrown Tally

If three or more of these sound familiar, it is time to evaluate an ERP like Business Central.

  1. Inventory never matches. Stock in Tally differs from the warehouse, and the gap is found only at month-end or audit.
  2. Production runs on Excel. BOMs, production plans, material requirements and job work are tracked in spreadsheets outside Tally.
  3. Multiple companies, multiple files. Each entity or branch has its own Tally data, and consolidation is a manual exercise every month.
  4. Month-end close takes too long. Finance spends days reconciling, re-keying and fixing entries before reports can be shared.
  5. No approval workflows. Purchase orders, discounts and payments are approved over WhatsApp or email, with no audit trail.
  6. Reports depend on one person. Management reports are rebuilt in Excel by the one person who knows how, and they are always a few days old.
  7. Remote access is a workaround. Teams use remote desktop, shared drives or data syncs to reach Tally from other locations.
  8. Integrations are fragile. E-commerce, CRM, EDI or banking connections rely on custom TDL scripts or manual imports.

The common thread: Tally is excellent accounting software, but these are operations problems. Solving them needs one connected system for finance, inventory, purchasing, sales and production.

Tally vs Business Central: Side-by-Side Comparison

Tally is built for accounting and compliance. Business Central is a full ERP that also includes accounting and Indian GST, TDS and TCS.

In short, Tally answers “what are my books?” Business Central answers “what is happening across my business right now?”

How Much Does Business Central Cost in India?

Business Central is licensed per user, per month, paid yearly. These are Microsoft’s current list prices for India (Microsoft pricing page), GST extra:

For comparison, TallyPrime Silver (single user) costs ₹22,500 for a lifetime licence or ₹9,000 a year on rental, plus GST (Tally pricing page). Tally is cheaper to license. The real comparison is the cost of the manual work, errors and add-ons that Tally needs as you grow.

Example licence costs (list price, before GST):

  • Distributor: 5 Essentials users + 10 Team Members = ₹39,925 a month, about ₹4.8 lakh a year.
  • Manufacturer: 8 Premium users + 15 Team Members = ₹83,175 a month, about ₹10 lakh a year.

Other costs to plan for:

  • Implementation: set-up, data migration, training and go-live support from a partner. The cost depends on modules, locations and customisation.
  • Add-on apps: for example, e-invoicing connectors, advanced manufacturing or industry apps.
  • Copilot Credits: AI agents such as the Sales Order Agent and Payables Agent need Copilot Credits, which are sold separately.
  • Support: an annual support plan for fixes, small changes and help after go-live.

There is no server, backup or upgrade cost in the cloud version. Microsoft hosts it and updates it twice a year.

How to Migrate from Tally to Business Central: 7 Steps

A well-run migration takes 8 to 16 weeks for most small and mid-sized companies. The timeline depends on the number of companies, locations, modules and how clean your Tally data is.

  1. Discovery and process mapping (weeks 1–2). Document how you buy, sell, stock, produce and close the books today. Identify what lives outside Tally, such as Excel sheets for production or approvals.
  2. Solution design (weeks 2–3). Map each process to Business Central. Define the chart of accounts, dimensions (branch, department, product line), GST and TDS set-up, number series and approval workflows.
  3. Data cleansing (weeks 3–5). Remove duplicate ledgers and items, fix GSTINs, PAN and HSN or SAC codes, and close or write off old open entries. This step decides how smooth go-live will be.
  4. Configuration and data migration (weeks 4–8). Set up the system and load masters and opening balances using configuration packages and Excel templates. Run a trial migration first and reconcile it against Tally.
  5. Testing (weeks 7–10). Run real scenarios end to end: purchase to payment, order to cash, production to dispatch, and a full GST return. Fix gaps before users see them.
  6. Training (weeks 9–11). Train each team on its own role centre and daily tasks, not the whole system. Short, role-based sessions work better than one long demo.
  7. Go-live and hypercare (weeks 10–16). Go live at the start of a month or quarter, load final balances, and run Tally in read-only mode for reference. Keep partner support close for the first month-end close.

Tip: Do not migrate in the middle of a GST return period. A month or quarter start keeps opening balances and returns clean.

What Data Moves from Tally, and What Stays

Most companies move masters, balances and open documents, not every historical voucher. Moving years of history adds cost and carries old errors into the new system.

Keep a read-only copy of Tally for at least the statutory record-keeping period, so auditors and tax queries can still be answered from the original data.

6 Common Tally Migration Mistakes (and How to Avoid Them)

  1. Copying Tally’s structure as it is. Tally ledgers often grow into hundreds of sub-ledgers for branches and cost centres. In Business Central, use a lean chart of accounts and dimensions instead.
  2. Skipping data cleansing. Duplicate customers, wrong GSTINs and missing HSN codes cause e-invoice and return errors after go-live. Clean data before loading it.
  3. Treating it as an accounting project only. The biggest gains come from inventory, purchase, production and approvals. Involve stores, production and sales from day one, not just finance.
  4. Over-customising. Business Central covers most needs out of the box. Rebuilding every Tally report or TDL feature adds cost and makes future updates harder.
  5. Weak GST testing. Test GST on every transaction type before go-live: intrastate, interstate, exports, reverse charge, credit notes, job work and e-way bills.
  6. Too little training. Users fall back to Excel when they are unsure. Train by role, give a short guide for daily tasks, and assign a power user in each team.

What Changes After the Switch: Manufacturing and Distribution

For manufacturers, the shift is from tracking production in Excel to planning it inside the ERP:

  • Multi-level production BOMs and routings give an accurate cost for every product: material, labour, machine time and overheads.
  • MRP tells purchase what to buy and production what to make, based on sales orders, forecasts and stock.
  • Subcontracting (job work) is tracked through purchase orders, with material sent to and received from job workers.
  • Capacity planning shows which work centres are overloaded before delays reach the customer.

Running more than one plant? Read our guide to ERP for multi-plant manufacturing.

For distributors and traders, the shift is from godown-level stock to full warehouse and order control:

  • Real-time stock across warehouses, with bins, batches, expiry dates and serial numbers.
  • Price lists, discounts and credit limits applied automatically on every order.
  • Purchase planning based on reorder points and actual sales, not guesswork.
  • EDI and Shopify connections to receive orders from retailers and marketplaces without re-keying.

For finance teams in both, month-end close gets faster because sales, purchase and stock post to the ledger as they happen. Multi-company consolidation, bank reconciliation and GST reports run from one system.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I migrate my data from Tally to Business Central?

Yes. Masters (ledgers, customers, vendors, items), opening balances, open invoices, opening stock and open orders are exported from Tally and loaded into Business Central using configuration packages and Excel templates.

How long does a Tally to Business Central migration take?

Most small and mid-sized companies go live in 8 to 16 weeks. Multi-company, multi-plant or heavily customised projects can take longer.

Does Business Central support Indian GST?

Yes. The India version of Business Central supports GST, TDS and TCS, and e-invoice and e-way bill can be generated through supported integrations.

Is Business Central more expensive than Tally?

The licence costs more: Essentials is ₹6,655 per user per month, against a one-time ₹22,500 for TallyPrime Silver. But Business Central replaces Excel trackers, add-ons and manual work, which often costs more than the licence difference.

Will we lose our old Tally data?

No. Keep Tally in read-only mode for history and audits. Many companies also load monthly summary balances so year-on-year reports work in Business Central.

Can Tally and Business Central run together?

Only for a short parallel run during go-live. Running both for long doubles data entry and causes mismatches.

Which Business Central licence do manufacturers need?

Premium (₹9,150 per user per month), because manufacturing (production orders, BOMs, routings, MRP and capacity planning) is included only in Premium. Users who only view or approve can use the ₹665 Team Members licence.

Is Business Central cloud or on-premises?

Both are available. Most new customers choose the cloud version, which Microsoft hosts, secures and updates twice a year.
