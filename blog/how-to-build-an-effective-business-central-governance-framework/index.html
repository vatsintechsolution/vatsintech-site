How to Build an Effective Business Central Governance Framework

What Is a Business Central Governance Framework?

Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central can bring finance, sales, purchasing, inventory, manufacturing, projects and other business processes together on a single ERP platform. As an organization grows, however, managing users, permissions, integrations, extensions, data and system changes becomes increasingly complex.

A Business Central governance framework provides a structured approach to managing these areas. It defines who can access the system, what users can do, how changes are approved, how business data is protected, and how the ERP environment is maintained over time.

Good governance is not about creating unnecessary restrictions. It is about maintaining the right balance between security, control, flexibility and business agility.

Why Does Business Central Governance Matter?

A Business Central environment can become significantly more complicated as a business expands. Organizations may add new companies, warehouses, manufacturing locations, users, third-party applications, integrations and custom extensions.

Without proper governance, businesses can face excessive user permissions, unauthorized changes, poor-quality data, undocumented integrations and unnecessary customization. These issues can increase security risks and make the ERP more difficult and expensive to maintain.

A governance framework helps organizations establish clear responsibilities and repeatable processes so that Business Central remains secure, scalable and aligned with business requirements.

Establish Clear Governance Roles

The first step is to define who is responsible for Business Central. Governance should involve both business and technical teams rather than being handled entirely by IT or an implementation partner.

A governance owner can coordinate policies and reviews, while business process owners manage areas such as finance, manufacturing, sales, purchasing and inventory. IT and security teams can manage access and environments, while developers and implementation partners handle approved technical changes.

Clearly defined responsibilities prevent confusion. For example, a developer can determine whether a customization is technically possible, but the relevant business owner should decide whether it is actually required.

Strengthen Business Central Security

Security should be one of the core elements of Business Central governance. Organizations should establish policies for user creation, access approval, permissions, privileged accounts, external access and periodic access reviews.

The least-privilege principle should be followed wherever possible. Users should receive only the permissions necessary to perform their responsibilities.

For example, an accounts payable employee may require access to vendor invoices and purchasing transactions but should not automatically receive system administration permissions.

A strong security model reduces the risk of unauthorized access while making it easier to identify and manage privileged users.

Use Role-Based Access Control

Role-Based Access Control, or RBAC, provides a practical way to organize Business Central user permissions. Instead of giving users broad access, organizations can create permission structures around specific job responsibilities.

A finance manager, sales employee, warehouse user and production planner will typically require different access.

Business Central supports security groups and permission sets, which can help organizations assign appropriate permissions to groups of users. Access should also be updated when employees change departments or responsibilities.

Regular reviews are important because permissions that were appropriate six months ago may no longer be appropriate today.

Manage Business Central Environments

Environment management is another important part of governance. Organizations should clearly define how development, testing, sandbox and production environments are used.

Development environments should be used for technical development, while sandbox environments can support testing and user acceptance. Production should be reserved for live business operations and protected with stricter access controls.

This separation reduces the risk of untested configuration changes, extensions or integrations affecting business operations.

A documented Business Central environment strategy also makes it easier to manage deployments, testing and future upgrades.

Govern Extensions and Customizations

Business Central offers extensive extensibility, but organizations should avoid customizing the platform unnecessarily.

Before developing a custom solution, businesses should determine whether the requirement can be addressed through standard Business Central functionality, configuration, an existing extension, an AppSource application or Power Platform.

Custom development should be considered when there is a clear business requirement that cannot be efficiently addressed through existing capabilities.

This approach helps reduce ERP technical debt and makes future upgrades easier.

Every important extension should also have a clear owner, documentation, testing process and maintenance plan.

Implement Business Central ALM

Application Lifecycle Management, or ALM, defines how Business Central applications and extensions move from development to production.

A typical process can include requirements gathering, development, code review, technical testing, user acceptance testing, approval and production deployment.

This process creates greater control over changes and reduces the possibility of untested code reaching production.

For larger Business Central environments, source control and structured deployment processes can further improve consistency and accountability.

Create a Change Management Process

ERP changes can affect multiple business processes. A modification to financial dimensions, approval workflows, inventory configuration or manufacturing processes may have consequences beyond the original requirement.

A Business Central change management process should therefore evaluate important changes before implementation.

Organizations should consider business impact, technical dependencies, security implications, data impact, testing requirements and rollback options.

Every significant change should have a clear reason, owner, approval and testing record.

Establish Data Governance

Business Central depends heavily on accurate and consistent data. Poor master data can affect reporting, inventory management, purchasing, production planning and financial decisions.

A Business Central data governance strategy should define ownership for customers, vendors, items, resources, bills of materials, locations and dimensions.

Businesses should establish standards for data creation, validation, naming conventions, duplicate prevention and corrections.

Data governance becomes especially important during Business Central data migration. Legacy data should be cleaned and validated before being transferred into the new ERP environment.

Govern Business Central Integrations

Modern businesses rarely use Business Central in isolation. It may connect with CRM systems, e-commerce platforms, banking applications, warehouse management systems, Power BI, Power Automate and external APIs.

Every integration should have clear ownership and documentation.

Organizations should know what data is exchanged, how authentication works, how frequently data is synchronized and what happens when an integration fails.

A proper Business Central integration governance process also defines how integration changes are tested and how errors are monitored.

Control External Partner Access

Implementation partners, consultants and third-party developers may require access to Business Central. However, external access should be carefully controlled.

Partners should receive only the permissions required for their responsibilities. Organizations should define who approves access, which environment can be accessed and when access should be removed.

Privileged access should receive additional scrutiny, particularly when external users have administrative capabilities.

When a project or support engagement ends, external accounts and permissions should be reviewed and removed where appropriate.

Include AI Governance

AI is becoming an important part of modern ERP platforms. Business Central users may increasingly use Copilot capabilities, AI-assisted processes, automated workflows and AI agents.

This introduces new governance considerations.

Organizations should determine which AI capabilities are being used, who can access them, what business information they can interact with and what actions require human approval.

As AI agents in ERP become more capable, businesses will need to include AI access, data security, human oversight and monitoring within their wider Business Central governance strategy.

Monitor and Review Business Central

Governance should not stop once policies are implemented. Organizations should continuously monitor their Business Central environment and review whether governance controls are working effectively.

Important areas to monitor can include permission changes, user accounts, administrative activity, extension deployments, integration failures, workflow changes and production modifications.

Businesses can also track governance KPIs such as excessive permissions, inactive accounts, failed deployments, unauthorized changes, integration errors and data-quality issues.

Regular reviews help identify problems before they become major operational or security risks.

Business Central Governance Best Practices

An effective governance strategy should focus on a few fundamental principles. Organizations should use least-privilege access, separate development and production environments, minimize unnecessary customization, document integrations and extensions, establish clear data ownership and review permissions regularly.

Significant ERP changes should be tested and approved before reaching production. External partner access should be controlled, and new Business Central features should be evaluated before being introduced into business-critical processes.

Most importantly, governance should remain practical. Excessively complicated policies can discourage users from following them. The goal should be clear accountability with minimal unnecessary friction.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Business Central governance framework?

A Business Central governance framework is a structured set of policies, responsibilities and processes used to manage security, users, data, environments, extensions, integrations, changes and compliance within Business Central.

Why is Business Central governance important?

Governance helps organizations control security risks, protect business data, manage user access, reduce technical debt and maintain a scalable ERP environment.

How should Business Central permissions be managed?

Permissions should be based on job responsibilities and the principle of least privilege. Security groups and permission sets can help organizations manage access consistently.

What is Business Central ALM?

Business Central ALM is the process of managing application and extension changes from development and testing through deployment and ongoing maintenance.

How can businesses reduce Business Central customization?

Businesses should first evaluate standard functionality, configuration, AppSource applications and Power Platform before developing custom solutions.

Should Business Central environments be separated?

Yes. Separating development, testing and production environments helps organizations test changes before they affect live operations.

Does Business Central governance include AI?

Yes. As businesses adopt AI capabilities and AI agents, governance should cover AI access, data permissions, human oversight, security and monitoring.

Final Thoughts

A strong Business Central governance framework provides the structure businesses need to manage their ERP environment securely while continuing to grow and innovate.

Security, user permissions, environment management, extensions, ALM, data governance, integrations and change management should work together rather than being treated as separate activities.

For organizations implementing Business Central, migrating from a legacy ERP or expanding their existing environment, governance should be considered from the beginning.
