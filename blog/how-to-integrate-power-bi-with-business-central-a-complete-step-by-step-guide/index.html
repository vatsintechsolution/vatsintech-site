How to Integrate Power BI with Business Central: A Complete Step-by-Step Guide

In today’s data-driven business environment, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central is a powerful ERP solution that manages your finance, sales, inventory, and operations in one place. But raw ERP data can be difficult to interpret without the right visualization tools.

That’s where Microsoft Power BI comes in. By integrating Power BI with Business Central, you can transform your business data into interactive, real-time dashboards that help you make smarter decisions.

In this guide, we’ll walk you through the entire integration process — from preparation to optimization.

Why Integrate Power BI with Business Central?

Integrating these two Microsoft tools offers a wide range of benefits:

  • Real-time insights: See up-to-date data without manual exports.

  • Better decision-making: Use visual dashboards to quickly spot trends and issues.

  • Single source of truth: Combine multiple data sources into one unified view.

  • Accessibility: Share dashboards securely with your team from anywhere.

Step 1: Prepare for Integration

Before starting, make sure you have:

  • An active Business Central license connected to your organization.

  • A Power BI account (Power BI Desktop for creating reports and Power BI Service for online sharing).

  • Appropriate user permissions in Business Central to access data tables.

Having these ready will ensure a smooth, error-free integration.

Step 2: Install and Open Power BI Desktop

Download the latest version of Power BI Desktop from the Microsoft website or Microsoft Store.
Once installed, open Power BI Desktop — this is where you’ll connect, design, and publish your Business Central dashboards.

Step 3: Connect Power BI to Business Central

  1. In Power BI Desktop, click Get Data from the Home ribbon.

  2. Search for and select Business Central as your data source.

  3. Sign in using your Microsoft work account linked to Business Central.

  4. Choose the correct environment (Production or Sandbox) and company you want to pull data from.

Step 4: Select and Load Data

You’ll see a list of available tables and datasets from Business Central.

  • Select the ones you need, such as Sales Orders, Customers, Inventory, or General Ledger Entries.

  • Click Load to import them into Power BI.

Pro Tip: Import only the data you need to keep your reports fast and focused.

Step 5: Build Your Dashboards

Once the data is loaded:

  • Use Power BI’s visual tools to create charts, tables, KPIs, and maps.

  • Combine multiple datasets to uncover cross-functional insights (e.g., sales performance by region).

  • Apply formatting to keep dashboards clean and easy to read.

Step 6: Set Up Data Refresh

To ensure your dashboards always display the latest figures:

  • Publish your report to Power BI Service.

  • In Power BI Service, configure a scheduled refresh (daily, hourly, or as required).

This eliminates the need for manual updates and ensures real-time decision-making.

Step 7: Secure and Optimize Your Reports

  • Use role-based security so users only see data relevant to them.

  • Remove unnecessary columns and tables to improve load times.

  • Review dashboard performance regularly to maintain efficiency.
