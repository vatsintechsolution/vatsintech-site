GST Rate Changes in Business Central: How to Update Tax Setup Without Errors

Introduction

When GST rates change, the ERP has to change with them, on the exact day. The GST Council’s 56th meeting on 3 September 2025 replaced the 5%, 12%, 18% and 28% structure with two main rates, 5% and 18%, plus a 40% special rate for luxury and sin goods, effective 22 September 2025 (PIB). Every business on Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central had to update its tax setup, and many found errors only after invoices had gone out.

Quick answer: In Business Central India, GST rates are set in Tax Types > GST > Tax Rates, where each rate is defined by HSN/SAC code, GST group, states and a Date From / Date To range. To change a rate without errors, do not edit the existing line. Instead, end-date the old rate on the day before the change, add a new line with the new rate starting on the effective date, update any HSN or GST group mappings on items, recheck open documents, and test before posting.

This guide walks through the setup, the exact steps, how to handle open orders and credit notes, and the errors to watch for.

How GST rates work in Business Central

Business Central India calculates GST through its Tax Engine. The rate on any document line is looked up from a combination of the line’s tax attributes, so a rate change has to be made in the right place and with the right dates.

The key fields on the Tax Rates page

According to Microsoft’s GST setup documentation, each rate line holds HSN/SAC, GST Group Code, From State, Location State Code, Date From, Date To, SGST, CGST and IGST, plus POS (place of supply) flags. Because every line carries a date range, Business Central can hold the old and new rate side by side, so a rate change becomes a dated change, not an overwrite.

Before you change anything: preparation checklist

Most GST rate errors in Business Central come from rushing the setup on the day of the change. Prepare a few days ahead.

  • Get the official notification and list the HSN/SAC codes whose rate changes, with old and new rates and the effective date
  • Export your current Tax Rates to Excel as a backup (Tax Engine supports export and import of configuration)
  • Run an item list by HSN/SAC and GST Group Code to see exactly which items are affected
  • Check whether any item now needs a different GST group. If the rate is reclassified rather than just changed, the group mapping changes too
  • List open sales orders, quotes, blanket orders and unposted invoices that will post after the effective date
  • Review price lists marked Price Inclusive of Tax, since the net price changes when the rate changes
  • Confirm with your CA how the time-of-supply rules apply to advances and invoices around the change date
  • Plan the change in a sandbox first, then repeat in production

A clear owner and change log for tax setup is part of good Business Central governance.

How to update GST rates in Business Central: step by step

The safe method is to end-date and add, never overwrite. Overwriting a rate line changes the history that past documents and reports rely on.

  1. Open the Tax Rates page. Search (Alt+Q) for Tax Types, select GST, then choose Tax Rates.
  2. Filter to the affected codes. Filter on HSN/SAC or GST Group Code so you only see the lines that change.
  3. End-date the old rate. On each affected line, set Date To to the day before the change. For a change effective 22 September 2025, that is 21 September 2025.
  4. Add the new rate line. Copy the line (same HSN/SAC, GST group, From State and Location State Code), set Date From to the effective date, leave Date To blank, and enter the new CGST, SGST and IGST percentages. For example, an 18% rate becomes CGST 9%, SGST 9% and IGST 18%.
  5. Repeat for every state combination. Rates are stored per state pair, so an HSN code used across several states and locations needs a new line for each combination that already exists.
  6. Update item mappings if codes were reclassified. Where goods move to a different group, change the GST Group Code on the item and then re-select the HSN/SAC Code. Business Central clears the HSN/SAC code when the group changes.
  7. Review compensation cess. Cess is set up separately under GST CESS. End-date any cess rates that no longer apply, as advised by your CA.
  8. Bulk changes: use Excel. For many codes, export the rate configuration, edit dates and rates in Excel, and import it back. Always import into a sandbox first.
  9. Test before go-live. Create test sales and purchase documents dated before and after the effective date, check the GST amounts in Statistics or the tax information factbox, and preview posting.
  10. Lock it down. Record what changed, who changed it and when, then restrict edit permission on Tax Rates to the finance admin.

Open orders, credit notes and prices: handling the transition

The setup is only half the job. Documents around the change date cause most of the trouble.

Open sales and purchase documents

Business Central picks the rate line whose date range covers the document, so an order entered before the change but posted after it may still show the old GST. Before posting, re-validate the lines (for example, re-enter the quantity or reopen and recalculate), then check the GST amount in Statistics. Do this as a batch review of open documents in the first days after the change.

Credit notes and returns against old invoices

A credit note for an invoice issued at the old rate should normally carry the old rate. In Business Central India, link the credit memo to the original invoice (Reference Invoice No.) and confirm the GST matches the original document before posting.

Price inclusive of tax

If sales prices are set as Price Inclusive of Tax, a rate change alters the net value and margin even though the shelf price stays the same. Decide whether to keep the MRP and absorb the difference, or revise the price list on the effective date.

Common errors after a GST rate change, and how to fix them

A 15-minute test before go-live

  • One B2B sales invoice, intra-state (CGST + SGST), dated after the change
  • One inter-state invoice (IGST), dated after the change
  • One invoice dated before the change, to confirm the old rate still applies
  • One purchase invoice, one credit memo against an old invoice, one advance receipt
  • Check the GST amounts, the posting preview and the e-invoice JSON

Frequently asked questions

Where are GST rates set up in Business Central?

In Tax Types > GST > Tax Rates. Each line defines the CGST, SGST and IGST percentages for an HSN/SAC code, GST group and state combination, valid between a Date From and a Date To.

How do I change a GST rate in Business Central?

End-date the existing rate line on the day before the change, then add a new line with the new rate and the effective date as Date From. Repeat for every state combination, update item mappings if needed, and test.

Should I edit the existing GST rate line?

No. Editing overwrites the history that past documents and reports use. Always end-date the old line and add a new one.

What happens to open sales orders when GST rates change?

They may still carry the old rate. Re-validate the lines before posting and check the GST amount in Statistics.

Which GST rate applies to a credit note for an old invoice?

Usually the rate on the original invoice. Link the credit memo to the original invoice and confirm the GST before posting.

Can I update many GST rates at once?

Yes. Export the Tax Engine rate configuration to Excel, update dates and rates, and import it, preferably into a sandbox first.

Do I need to change HSN codes when GST rates change?

Only if goods are reclassified into a different group. Changing an item’s GST Group Code clears its HSN/SAC code, so select it again.

Conclusion

A GST rate change takes minutes to configure in Business Central but can cost weeks of corrections if done in a hurry. Plan ahead: end-date instead of overwriting, cover every state combination, recheck open documents and test before go-live. Then the next rate change becomes a routine task, not a fire drill.
