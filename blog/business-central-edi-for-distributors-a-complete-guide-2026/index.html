Business Central EDI for Distributors: A Complete Guide (2026)

Introduction

Distributors that sell to large retailers, e-commerce marketplaces and OEMs cannot run on emailed PDFs and manual order entry for long. Big buyers expect purchase orders, shipment notices and invoices to move system to system, in their format, within hours. Miss a field or a deadline and the penalty arrives as a chargeback.

Quick answer: Business Central EDI is the electronic exchange of business documents (purchase orders, acknowledgements, advance ship notices and invoices) between Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central and a trading partner’s system in a standard format such as ANSI X12 or EDIFACT. Business Central has no built-in EDI module, so distributors connect it through an EDI app from Microsoft AppSource or a managed EDI provider. Incoming orders then become sales orders automatically, and shipments and invoices go back to the partner without re-keying.

This guide explains how EDI works inside Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central, which documents distributors exchange, how to implement it and how to avoid the most common compliance mistakes.

What is EDI and why do distributors need it?

EDI (Electronic Data Interchange) is a standard way for two companies’ systems to exchange business documents without paper, email or re-typing. Each document follows a fixed structure, so the receiving system can read and post it automatically.

For a distributor, EDI matters for four reasons:

  • Retailers require it. Large retail chains, marketplaces and OEMs often onboard only suppliers who can trade electronically.
  • Volume outgrows manual entry. Hundreds of purchase order lines a day cannot be keyed into an ERP without delays and errors.
  • Compliance has a price. Late or inaccurate shipment notices, wrong labels and invoice mismatches lead to chargebacks and deductions.
  • Speed wins orders. Partners that receive acknowledgements and shipment notices quickly prefer suppliers who make their receiving easy.

Does Business Central support EDI natively?

No. Business Central ships with strong sales, purchasing, inventory and warehouse features, but it has no built-in EDI translator or partner network. Distributors add EDI in one of three ways:

Most distributors choose an AppSource app or a managed provider, because the partner “maps” (each retailer’s document rules) are maintained for them. Our list of third-party apps that integrate with Business Central explains how to evaluate extensions. For lighter, non-EDI automations, Power Automate can move data between Business Central and other systems.

Key EDI documents every distributor should know

Most retail trading relationships run on six to eight documents. ANSI X12 codes are common with North American partners; EDIFACT names are used more in Europe, the Middle East and many global buyers.

The ASN (856 / DESADV) causes the most chargebacks, because it must match the physical shipment carton by carton and arrive before the goods do.

Because the ASN and invoice come from posted shipments rather than spreadsheets, what the retailer receives matches what was packed. Distributors that also ship with their own fleet can extend this flow with D365 transportation management.

Benefits of EDI in Business Central for distributors

The main benefit is fewer chargebacks: documents are built from posted Business Central data, so what you send matches what you shipped.

  • No re-keying of orders. Inbound POs become sales orders in minutes, with customer item numbers translated to your item numbers.
  • Accurate ASNs. Shipment notices are generated from the posted warehouse shipment, including carton contents and SSCC labels.
  • Faster cash. Invoices go out the moment the shipment posts, which shortens days sales outstanding.
  • Partner scorecards improve. On-time acknowledgements, ASNs and invoices raise your compliance score with retailers.
  • Scale without hiring. New trading partners add maps, not data-entry staff. See business processes you should automate before hiring more employees.
  • Real-time visibility. Order, shipment and invoice status sit in one system, ready for Power BI dashboards on Business Central.

How to implement EDI in Business Central: 7 steps

A typical EDI rollout for a distributor takes a few weeks per trading partner wave, depending on the number of partners and documents.

  1. List trading partners and documents. For each partner, record the standard (X12 or EDIFACT), version, documents and connection method (VAN, AS2, SFTP or API).
  2. Clean master data first. Set up item references for customer item numbers and barcodes, units of measure, ship-to codes and customer price lists. Our guide to managing inventory in Business Central covers the item setup.
  3. Choose the EDI approach. Compare AppSource apps and managed providers on partner coverage, pricing per document and support.
  4. Configure warehouse processes. Use warehouse shipments, picks and packing so carton-level data exists for the ASN.
  5. Map and test each document. Run sample POs, ASNs and invoices with each partner until they certify you.
  6. Train staff on exceptions. Teams should handle rejected orders, price variances and failed acknowledgements from a single error queue.
  7. Go live in waves and monitor. Start with your highest-volume partner, then add others. Track errors and partner scorecards weekly.

EDI readiness checklist

  • Trading partner list with EDI standards and versions
  • Item references set up for every partner item number and UPC/EAN
  • Ship-to addresses and store/DC codes mapped
  • Warehouse shipments and packing enabled
  • GS1 company prefix and SSCC label printing in place
  • Exception owner named for rejected or failed documents
  • Test sign-off from each partner before go-live

EDI for distributors in India

Indian distributors supplying modern retail chains, quick-commerce platforms, exporters’ buyers and global OEMs increasingly receive EDI or API-based order requirements. EDI also works alongside GST compliance: Business Central’s India localisation handles GST, and the EDI invoice should carry the same invoice number and values as the GST e-invoice registered on the government Invoice Registration Portal (IRP). Keeping both generated from one posted invoice avoids mismatches. Vatsin supports trading and distribution businesses and retail suppliers across India and the US.

Frequently asked questions

Does Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central have built-in EDI?

No. Business Central has no native EDI module. You add EDI through an AppSource extension, a managed EDI provider or a custom integration built on Business Central APIs.

What EDI documents does a distributor need?

Most distributors need the 850 purchase order, 855 acknowledgement, 856 advance ship notice, 810 invoice and 997 functional acknowledgement. Some retailers also require the 860 PO change and 846 inventory advice.

How does EDI reduce retailer chargebacks?

EDI builds the ASN, labels and invoice from the same posted shipment data in Business Central. That keeps documents on time and matched to the physical goods, which removes the main causes of compliance penalties.

How long does a Business Central EDI implementation take?

A single trading partner with standard documents can often go live in a few weeks. Timelines grow with the number of partners, the quality of master data and each partner’s testing process.

Can small distributors afford EDI on Business Central?

Yes. Many EDI providers price per trading partner or per document, so small distributors can start with one key retailer. Combine this with the right Business Central licence to keep costs predictable.

Is EDI the same as GST e-invoicing in India?

No. EDI exchanges documents with your trading partners, while GST e-invoicing registers invoices with the government IRP. Both should come from the same posted Business Central invoice.

Can EDI work with my e-commerce and marketplace channels?

Yes. EDI usually covers large retail partners, while marketplace and web-store orders come in through connectors. See our guide on Business Central integration with Shopify and WooCommerce.

Conclusion: make EDI a growth tool, not a compliance cost

For distributors, EDI in Business Central turns retailer requirements into an advantage: orders flow in without typing, shipments and invoices go out on time, and chargebacks fall. The key is clean master data, warehouse discipline and a partner who has done it before.
